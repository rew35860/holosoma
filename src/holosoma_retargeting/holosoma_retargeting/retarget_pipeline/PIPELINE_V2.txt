Pipeline v2: direct coupled arm-and-finger refinement
====================================================

Branch: feature/wuji-pipeline-v2
Existing v1 behavior remains the default. Select --pipeline-version v2 explicitly.

Full prepared-input pipeline (omniretarget environment, from the package root):

  python retarget_pipeline/pipeline.py --pipeline-version v2 --stage all \
    --task-name carrying_dining_chair_with_both_hands_and_back-660 \
    --object-name dining_chair --data-path /path/to/prepared/inputs \
    --save-dir /path/to/new/output

Inputs: <sequence>.pt and <sequence>_handkp.npy, same as v1. The object's full
G1/Wuji collision model and source mesh must be present in models/. Pipeline
stages part0 and part1 can be reused with --pipeline-version v2 --stage part2.

1. OmniRetarget supplies the complete body trajectory.
2. Quick finger-only IK maps the human's anatomical finger directions into the
   robot palm frame, respecting all 20 finger joint limits per hand. Six short
   iterations per frame, with sequential warm starts. There is no floating-hand
   world-position optimization and no object-interaction optimization at this step.
3. One coupled solve optimizes both arms and both hands: 14 arm/wrist coordinates
   plus 40 finger coordinates. The base, waist, legs and object remain exactly
   equal to the OmniRetarget result at every frame.
4. The final refined coordinates are exported directly. No v1 contact/release
   blend, no restoration of initial finger angles, and no post-hoc smoothing.

Outputs: v2/finger_initialization.npz, v2/final_v2.npz (30 Hz),
v2/v2_wuji_50hz.npz (tracker format), v2/run.json, v2/iterations.json,
and v2/status.json. Resampling is only for the tracker export; evaluate at 30 Hz.

Human hand representation
-------------------------
The mapper accepts 21 world-space landmarks per hand in explicit order:
wrist, then thumb/index/middle/ring/pinky, each MCP/PIP/DIP/tip.
HUMOTO's existing inputs are Mixamo forward-kinematic landmarks, not MANO pose
coefficients. The pilot uses these directly. MANO-derived landmarks can use the
same mapper after conversion to this explicit order and the canonical scene frame.
Raw MANO axis-angle coefficients are NOT accepted as joint angles or landmarks.
No new MANO/SMPL-X fitting or model dependency is introduced for HUMOTO.

Objective and collision handling
--------------------------------
The interaction-mesh weight remains 500/N, with exponential distance weights and
fingertip emphasis 10. New v2 terms include world-space fingertip tracking,
source-contact surface targets, palm-axis alignment, a weak quick-finger prior,
and a contact-dependent Omni arm prior. Anatomical finger directions and temporal
smoothness are included. All resolved values are saved in each run.json.

Collision constraints cover the moving arms/hands against the object, ground and
other robot geometry, including cross-hand collisions and nonadjacent fingers.
Self pairs respect model collision masks/exclusions and the existing structural
ancestor filter. Object collision queries do not rely on the old body-stage
collision bitmask. Bounding-sphere pruning is conservative; native MuJoCo signed
geometry distances and analytic relative point Jacobians supply constraints.

The target tolerance is 1 mm. Penalized emergency slack keeps inherited
infeasible poses solvable; residual collisions are reported, not hidden or
classified as collision-free. Fixed-body collisions cannot be corrected by a
refinement constrained to leave the body unchanged.

Numerical changes from v1 are intentional, not claimed to be algebraically the
same retargeter: 54-DOF coupled solve, new initialization/objective terms, all
relevant moving-chain collision constraints, native Clarabel matrix assembly,
line search on the nonlinear merit, and convergence-based stopping. Each frame
has at most 15 iterations (first frame 40); minimum 3 and step tolerance 2e-4.
There is no per-frame eight-iteration warmup optimization.

This directly assembles a convex quadratic objective and conic constraints for
Clarabel, avoiding CVXPY canonicalization in the optimization loop. Unit checks
compare the native subproblem with an independent CVXPY formulation and verify
kinematic and signed-distance Jacobians by finite differences.

Matched pilot and evaluation
---------------------------
Four complete HUMOTO clips were selected before v2 results: dining-chair carry,
one-hand low-chair carry, woven-basket lift, and clothes-rack dragging. They are
a development pilot, not a held-out benchmark or a claim of dataset-wide gains.

The exact archived body trajectory is reused for paired quality comparisons.
OmniRetarget is also rerun independently to measure body-stage runtime and check
repeatability. The reported sequential time is body + v2 process + 50-Hz export;
the independent timing rerun and refinement may execute concurrently in the pilot.
Input extraction, rendering and evaluation are reported separately/excluded from
retargeting time. V1 historical timings were under a different concurrent load,
so speedup is an observed workstation comparison, not a controlled hardware study.

Contact masks, source target positions, collision geometry, foot masks and all
quality definitions are copied unchanged from evaluation_v1. Combined penetration
uses object >20 mm OR ground/self >10 mm, and conditional frame-max depth over
violating frames, as documented in evaluation_penetration_v2. These are CoACD
collision measurements and inferred source contacts, not an exact DexWeave metric
implementation. Both v1 and v2 use identical geometry and masks in this pilot.

Sampling:
  The production preparation requests 100 object points, overriding the helper
  function's default of 50. It calls trimesh.sample.sample_surface_even with seed
  42. That sampler generates candidates and removes points closer than its
  spacing radius, so fewer than 100 may remain. Actual counts were dining chair
  71, low chair 60, woven basket 90, clothes rack 90, table 58, organizer 56.
  These are fixed, cached object-local arrays, reused by both versions at every
  frame. They are NOT random choices among those counts, and the object is NOT
  resampled during optimization. They define interaction-mesh nodes; collision
  checks use native geometry against CoACD pieces, not these sparse samples.

Non-penetration:
  phi = signed distance (positive outside, negative in penetration).
  Linearized constraint: phi(q) + J_phi*dq + s >= -tau, s >= 0.
  Collision objective: (w_s / 2) * sum(s^2).
  V2 tau=0.001 m and w_s=100000; collision_weight is exactly this w_s.
  Example: a predicted 10 mm penetration needs at least 9 mm slack, giving
  0.5 * 100000 * 0.009^2 = 4.05 objective units for that collision pair.
  No finite b hard bound is applied. The legacy non-emergency configuration
  additionally imposed phi >= -b and s <= b-tau, with b=0.03 m. However the
  actual ref_candidates runs enabled pen_emergency=True, removing both bounds.
  V2 retains that unbounded-slack behavior, with broader collision coverage.
  Its 25 mm collision detection radius is a positive-distance query cutoff, not
  a maximum permitted penetration. The old hand solver queried within 100 mm.
  A large penalty is not a guarantee: organizer v2 frame 140 has a measured
  34.01 mm right-ring-finger/object collision. No threshold or output was changed
  to hide it. See residual_collisions.json and per-frame metrics.

Solver differences:
  Both use Clarabel. V1 builds CVXPY expressions and canonicalizes each convex
  subproblem, solving floating hands and later attached arms/fingers per side.
  V2 directly builds numeric quadratic/conic matrices for one 54-joint solve.
  V1 uses up to 8 bone warmup + 15 main iterations per frame in each expensive
  pass; v2 uses a separate 6-iteration finger mapping and one main solve, capped
  at 15 iterations (40 on frame 0). V2 stops on accepted step norm <2e-4 after
  at least three iterations, or stops when line search finds no improving step.
  V2 line search tries step factors 1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 using the nonlinear full
  objective plus collision penalty; the old baseline does not enable that.
  Old trust norm cap: 0.3 per separate subproblem; v2: 0.45 over 54 joint angles.
  Old attached arm/finger rate bound: 0.12 rad/frame; v2: 0.20 rad/frame at 30 Hz.
  V1 arm refinement starts at a contact anchor and traverses forward/backward;
  v2 traverses forward from the first frame with previous-pose warm starts.

E_direction:
  For each finger, normalize (tip - MCP), then penalize squared difference
  between the robot and human vectors in the object frame. It controls the
  whole finger's pointing direction, independently of length and global position.
  It does not fully constrain curl: several joint bends can yield the same
  MCP-to-tip direction. The old current pipeline left w_dir=0; it was an available
  feature, not an active term. V2 has no separate MCP-to-tip E_direction term;
  it matches each of the 15 anatomical finger-segment directions in palm frames.

Additional v2 terms:
  Fingertip tracking (25): all ten robot tip proxies follow the corresponding
    source world-space tips. Controls placement even outside source contact.
  Source-contact attraction (100): on source finger-contact frames (source tip
    within 30 mm of the original object mesh), pull the robot tip toward the
    nearest object-surface point to that source fingertip. Encourages contact at
    the demonstrated region; targets never come from an optimized floating hand.
  Palm-axis alignment (0.5): align the palm's wrist-to-middle-knuckle axis and
    palm normal to their human counterparts. Helps orient the palm around grasped
    objects even if fingertip position alone permits an unwanted wrist rotation.
  Finger initialization prior (0.005): weakly retain the quickly mapped human
    finger angles where contact/pose objectives underconstrain the solution.
  Omni arm prior (0.002 contact / 0.05 no-contact): retain the original body
    retargeter's arm pose; relax the pull when the source hand contacts the object.
  Temporal term (0.15): penalize changes of all optimized angles from the previous
    refined frame, reducing jitter with a possible lag/accuracy tradeoff.
  Weight magnitudes alone cannot rank term importance: residual units, numbers
    of residuals and interaction-mesh normalization differ across terms.

The core model keeps the original interaction-mesh fingertip emphasis, main bone
term, temporal smoothness, quick finger initialization, numeric damping, limits,
trust region and collision constraints. It is not a V1 rerun. In particular,
removing the finger prior does NOT remove quick initialization or the bone term.
No Omni body stage or V1 stage is rebuilt. All variants reuse the archived body.

Settings JSONs and input hashes are frozen before the first result. The solver
file is unchanged; the same zero-weight residuals still compute their geometry.
This cleanly measures objective removal, but is not a runtime benchmark of code
with the unused calculations deleted. Times cover preparation, initialization,
joint refinement and Python subprocess overhead; body/evaluation/rendering and
50-Hz tracker resampling are excluded. Full V2 is rerun for paired timing and
checked against the original pilot trajectory. Every run checks identical quick
initialization, identical source contacts, fixed remaining body, finite output,
joint limits and the 0.20 rad/frame rate cap.

Evaluation uses the unchanged pilot adapter and frozen metric implementation.
Report plain and penetration-gated F1, recall/precision, contact gap, penetration
duration/depth, primary/secondary/palm errors, body error and jerk, hand slip and
timing. Undefined conditional depth remains null with contributing clip count.
Use paired per-clip changes as well as macro averages. Do not infer physical
grasp success or necessity of every term from proximity F1 alone.

No-fingertip is the best simpler candidate in this development study, not a new
production default: contact and fingertip errors rise slightly, jerk also rises,
while palm/body error and penetration improve. It retains all four other added
terms plus the interaction-mesh fingertip emphasis. The earlier hypothesis that
the finger prior was the easiest redundant term was not supported: removal loses

Palm alignment clearly helps palm orientation; source-contact attraction helps
contact, especially for clothes rack. Arm-prior removal has small average effects
but more penetration events and a numerical failure. These failures do not prove
mathematical necessity, nor physical infeasibility of the source motion.

Objective audit and runtime interpretation (2026-10-18)
------------------------------------------------------
Run src/wuji_wbt/tools/pipeline_v2_ablation/audit_objectives.py against the saved
experiment caches to reproduce objective_audit.json, objective_audit_per_clip.csv
and objective_audit.txt in the ablation output directory. This uses all 39
successful cached runs without rerunning optimization or changing metric definitions.

The contact target is the original triangle-surface point nearest the HUMAN tip,
held fixed within each frame's optimization. It is not the point nearest the
current robot tip. Interaction-mesh neighbours and weights rebuild from source
geometry each frame; contact labels do not switch their weights or connections.
On the shared five clips, contact-labelled human tips are 10.24 mm from their
projected surface targets on average. Attraction therefore changes the target
as well as increasing its weight relative to direct source-tip tracking.

Full V2 shared-five proximity F1 is 98.55 percent, but 1 mm penetration-gated F1
is only 0.08 percent. Mean frame-maximum hand/object depth is 1.011 mm; pooled
frame median/max are 1.055/1.668 mm. A hand's >1 mm collision invalidates all five
predicted contacts for that hand. Mean fingertip-to-surface distance on source
contact samples is still 14.54 mm. The unchanged coarse 0.43 percent penetration
duration does not establish precise, penetration-free grasping. Organizer is
excluded from this shared-five comparison because three variants failed; its
full-V2 maximum hand penetration is 34.01 mm. These findings qualify the earlier
simplification recommendation; no production default was changed.

The selected four V1 clips average 1679.90 seconds floating-hand optimization and
3770.67 seconds recorded preprocessing/retarget/export/render subtotal. Low chair
and woven basket are the slowest and fourth-slowest clips in the 120-clip batch.
The corresponding all-120 subtotal average is 2190.83 seconds; including validation
and video checks it is 2205.73 seconds (36 min 46 s). Dining-chair raw preprocessing
is unmeasured. These four clips are not representative of dataset-wide runtime.

Both versions use Clarabel. A short V1 profile attributed 76.06 percent of sampled
time to CVXPY compilation and 2.35 percent to numerical solving. V2 bypasses that
compilation and also changes stages, objectives and stopping rules. The four V2
clips used 8091 coupled subproblems over 1061 frames; 471 frames stopped because
line search found no improving step, so early termination is not always convergence.
There is no controlled implementation-only benchmark attributing the full speedup
to direct matrices. Generated trajectories, datasets and videos remain local.
